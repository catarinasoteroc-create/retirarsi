Ayurveda: An Introduction to the Doshas and Your Constitution

A 5,000-year-old framework for understanding your own nature — why you respond the way you do, what throws you off balance, and how to come back to yourself.

By Catarina  ·  October 2026  ·  8 min read

The Sanskrit word Ayurveda means, literally, the knowledge of life: ayur = life, veda = knowledge. What emerged from that name, over five thousand years in India, is one of the oldest and most sophisticated systems of natural medicine we have — not just a collection of remedies, but a complete philosophy of how to live in a way that maintains health and balance.

I came to Ayurveda through herbalism, and what struck me most when I first studied it was this: it is a system fundamentally built around the individual. It doesn't ask, "what is good for people?" It asks, "what is good for this particular person, with this particular constitution, in this particular season of life?" That distinction matters enormously — because what balances one person can disturb another.

"The primary goal of Ayurveda is the maintenance of balance. The secondary goal is rebalancing what has gone out of balance — so it can be maintained."

The five elements and the three doshas

Ayurveda holds that everything in the universe — including the human body — is composed of five elements: ether (space), air, fire, water and earth. These five elements combine into three primary patterns of energy, called the doshas. The doshas are present in every cell, tissue and organ of the body, governing all biological and psychological functions.

We are all born with all three doshas — but in different proportions. This unique combination is called your prakriti, your basic constitution. Most people have one or two dominant doshas. Understanding which they are, and what throws them out of balance, is the first step in Ayurvedic self-knowledge.

When the doshas are in balance — in their natural proportion for you — you function well: good energy, clear thinking, stable mood, sound digestion, sound sleep. When they are out of balance (called vikriti), the effects show up physically, emotionally and mentally.

Vata

Air + Ether

Cold · Light · Mobile · Dry · Subtle · Rough

Vata is the energy of movement. It governs all movement in the body: breathing, circulation, the nervous system, elimination. It moves the diaphragm, the muscles, the limbs. It stimulates the intellect.

In balance

  • Comfortable movement
  • Regular breathing
  • Consistent appetite
  • Good energy
  • Calm, creative mind
  • Enthusiasm and inspiration

Out of balance

  • Anxiety, fear, loneliness
  • Insomnia, restless sleep
  • Irregular digestion
  • Weight loss, dehydration
  • Pain, cramps, numbness
  • Very dry skin and hair
What disrupts Vata

Irregular routines, erratic mealtimes, cold and dry weather, excessive mental work, travel, bitter or astringent foods.

What restores Vata

Warmth, regularity, stability — consistent sleep and meal times, contact with water (walks by the sea or river), gentle oil massage with sesame oil, warm and nourishing foods, meditation, time in nature.

Pitta

Fire + Water

Hot · Intense · Sharp · Light · Liquid

Pitta is the energy of transformation. It governs digestion, metabolism, body temperature, hormone levels, visual perception, hunger and thirst, the quality of the skin. It is responsible for liver function, bile production, and the clarity of perception — both physical and mental.

In balance

  • Healthy appetite and thirst
  • Sharp intelligence
  • Courage and flexibility
  • Radiant skin
  • Charisma, warmth
  • Strong vision and focus

Out of balance

  • Irritability, anger
  • Skin irritation, rashes
  • Premature greying
  • Excessive heat, inflammation
  • Digestive problems, diarrhoea
  • Burnout from overworking
What disrupts Pitta

Excessive heat or sun exposure, alcohol and tobacco, overactivity, spicy, salty or acidic foods, skipping meals, deadline pressure.

What restores Pitta

Cooling, slowing down — not skipping meals, favouring cool foods, spending time laughing, contact with nature (parks, gardens, birdsong), swimming in a river, cooling oil massages, walking in moonlight.

Kapha

Water + Earth

Cold · Wet · Heavy · Solid · Slow · Stable

Kapha is the energy of structure and nourishment. It stores the energy that Vata brings and Pitta manages. Its cohesive nature gives the body stability, while its fluid qualities lubricate the organs. Kapha governs physical strength, immunity, growth and the stability of the mind.

In balance

  • Stable, affectionate, calm
  • Excellent endurance
  • Deep, peaceful sleep
  • Compassion and patience
  • Strong immunity
  • Groundedness

Out of balance

  • Congestion, wet cough
  • Excessive sleep, lethargy
  • Very slow digestion
  • Weight gain, water retention
  • Allergies, oily skin
  • Resistance to change
What disrupts Kapha

Oversleeping, overeating, insufficient movement, cold and damp weather, heavy, fatty, sweet or salty foods.

What restores Kapha

Movement and stimulation — vigorous activity, staying warm, clearing what is no longer needed, mountain walks, dancing, watching the sunset, dry or vigorous massage with safflower oil.

Arrábida has elements of all three doshas: the movement of the sea (Vata), the fire of the sun on limestone (Pitta), the rootedness of ancient earth and forest (Kapha).

What this means in practice

Knowing your dominant dosha — or the dosha that is currently out of balance — gives you practical guidance that is specific to you, rather than generic wellness advice. Someone who is predominantly Vata will be thrown off by exactly the things that a Kapha type needs: travel, irregularity, cold environments. What restores a Pitta might overstimulate a Vata.

This is one of the reasons I find Ayurveda so useful alongside inner cartography and somatic work: it gives a language for the patterns of imbalance that show up in the body, and it points toward the specific adjustments — in food, in rhythm, in environment, in movement — that bring someone back to their own natural equilibrium.

Ayurveda is also deeply seasonal. The qualities of each season correspond to the qualities of the doshas: autumn and early winter increase Vata (cold, dry, mobile); late spring and summer increase Pitta (hot, intense); late winter and spring can aggravate Kapha (cold, damp, heavy). Adjusting food, routine and care practices with the seasons — rather than living the same way year-round — is one of Ayurveda's core insights.

Ayurveda and skin care: a brief note

Ayurveda has detailed traditions around skin care that go far beyond what most people know. Each dosha type calls for a different approach — different oils, different cleansers, different rhythms of care. Vata skin (typically dry and sensitive) needs warming and nourishing oils: sesame, almond, avocado, with essential oils of geranium, neroli, orange. Pitta skin (sensitive, prone to redness) needs cooling: sweet almond oil, rose essential oil, sandalwood, chamomile hydrosol. Kapha skin (oilier, prone to congestion) needs lightening and stimulating: jojoba, sunflower oil, lavender and bergamot essential oils.

The recipes that come from this tradition — face cleansers made from flours and herbs, facial masks from fruit pulp, facial oils blended according to constitution — are among the most effective and gentle approaches to skin care I've encountered. And they connect you to a very different relationship with your own body: one of attentiveness rather than correction.

"Ayurveda doesn't tell you what to do. It tells you who you are — and trusts you to know what to do with that."

Where to begin

The first step is learning your constitution — which combination of doshas is your natural baseline. There are many dosha quizzes online; they give a rough starting point, though a practitioner assessment goes much deeper. From there, the most impactful changes tend to be the simplest: adjusting to seasonal eating, establishing a more regular daily rhythm, adding an oil massage (self-massage, called abhyanga in Ayurveda, is one of the most effective practices for nervous system regulation across all three doshas).
